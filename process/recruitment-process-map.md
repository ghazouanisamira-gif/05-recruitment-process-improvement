# Recruitment Process Map

## End-to-End Operating Flow

**Workforce Need → Recruitment Planning → Sourcing & Acquisition → Screening → Interview → Selection → Training Readiness → Onboarding → Early Retention → Performance Review**

## Process Architecture

| Stage | Management Question | Key Output | Example KPI | Main Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Workforce Need | What capacity is required? | Validated recruitment requirement | Demand volume | Requirement validation |
| Planning | What must be delivered and when? | Recruitment plan | Planned vs. delivered | Planning review |
| Sourcing & Acquisition | Which channels can generate the required flow? | Candidate pipeline | Source volume / quality | Channel monitoring |
| Screening | Are candidates meeting defined criteria? | Qualified shortlist | Screening conversion | Screening criteria |
| Interview | Are evaluations consistent? | Assessment result | Interview conversion | Evaluation framework |
| Selection | Is the selected profile aligned with the need? | Selected candidate | Selection rate | Approval / validation |
| Training Readiness | Is the candidate prepared for the next stage? | Training-ready cohort | Readiness / attendance | Handover control |
| Onboarding | Is the transition operationally ready? | Onboarded employee | Completion rate | Recruitment–Training–Operations handoff |
| Early Retention | Is the recruitment outcome sustainable? | Retained employee | Early attrition | Follow-up |
| Performance Review | What needs to change? | Decision / action plan | KPI trend | Review and escalation |

## Data & Flow Management

The process was not managed only through a visual process map. Candidate-flow data was collected and structured to understand:

- volume entering each stage
- conversion between stages
- losses between stages
- lead times
- sourcing-channel contribution
- quality of candidates progressing through the funnel
- onboarding completion
- early retention outcomes

This allowed recruitment decisions to move from activity reporting toward **flow analysis and evidence-based action**.

## Sourcing & Acquisition

The sourcing layer considered the interaction between:

**Channels + Recruitment Actions + Communication + Marketing / Acquisition**

Channel performance was assessed through the quality and progression of the resulting candidate flow, not only through raw volume.

## Capability & Handoffs

Critical interfaces included:

- Operations → Recruitment
- Recruitment → Candidate
- Recruitment → Training
- Recruitment → HR / onboarding
- Recruitment → Operations
- Recruitment ↔ Marketing / Communication where acquisition support was required

## Capability Development

Recruitment roles were supported through structured development covering:

- role and métier knowledge
- recruitment techniques
- candidate assessment
- communication
- evaluation consistency
- follow-up
- reporting and data interpretation

Training effectiveness was followed through:

**Training → Application → Observation → Evaluation → Feedback → Coaching → Follow-up**

## Diagnostic Questions

- Is workforce demand defined early enough?
- Which channels generate the required candidate flow?
- Where does candidate flow slow down?
- Where are candidates lost?
- Which criteria create variation?
- Are handoffs documented?
- Are recruitment roles sufficiently trained?
- Are training needs identified from observed gaps?
- Is post-training application evaluated?
- Does the KPI measure activity, quality or outcome?
- What happens when performance deviates?

## Improvement Logic

**Map → Collect → Analyze → Clarify Ownership → Develop Capability → Standardize → Measure → Improve → Control**
